# Legal notes

This is general information, not legal advice. When in doubt about your
situation, ask a lawyer.

## What gb2swf contains

- No games and no ROMs.
- No Nintendo code: no boot ROM, no BIOS, no firmware, and no copy of the
  Nintendo logo from the cartridge header. gb2swf starts every game in the state
  the console is in after its own start-up, without running Nintendo's code.
- Its own emulator runtime and tools (MIT license) and the third-party software
  listed in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.

## Which ROMs you may use

- **Dumps of cartridges you own.** Make the copy yourself, from your own
  cartridge, for your own use (for example with a cartridge reader).
- **Homebrew and other games whose license allows it.** Many homebrew games are
  free to copy; check the license of each game. `docs/compatibility.md` lists the
  ones we tested, with their licenses.

**Netherlands and the European Union:** downloading a copy of a game from an
illegal source is not allowed, also not when you own the original. The Court of
Justice of the EU ruled this in 2014 (ACI Adam and Others, case C-435/12). Owning
the cartridge does not make a download from a ROM site legal.

## What you make with gb2swf

A SWF made by gb2swf contains the game you supplied. The rights to that game stay
with whoever holds them (usually the publisher or the author). gb2swf gives you
no rights to the game.

- Keep SWFs of commercial games you own for your own use. Do not publish,
  upload, share or sell them.
- For homebrew, publish only what the game's license allows, and follow its
  conditions (for example: credit the authors, include the license text, or offer
  the game's source code when it is GPL-licensed).
